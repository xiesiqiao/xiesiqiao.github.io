Postdoctoral Associate, Institute of Behavioral Science (IBS), University of Colorado Boulder  
Ph.D. in Geography, Arizona State University

Hi, I'm **Siqiao** (谢斯峤). I'm a computational social scientist and population geographer. I study how neighborhoods, their built form, infrastructure, and socioeconomic conditions, shape population health. I build longitudinal, neighborhood-level datasets and GIS/machine-learning pipelines to track change over time, explain health-place links, and turn analysis into action.

### 🏥 Neighborhood health, built from the block up
I build neighborhood-scale, longitudinal data systems: harmonized small-area units, crosswalks, and indicators that let us follow places and people across years. Using GIS, machine learning, remote sensing, and administrative statistics, I connect built form, infrastructure, and socioeconomic conditions to population health across the life course, including midlife mortality, mental health, and healthy longevity. The goal is simple: show how place makes people vulnerable or resilient, track that change clearly, and identify which local actions are worth targeting.

### 🔄 From GeoHealth research to action
I work across universities, public agencies, NGOs, and the private sector to turn analysis into decisions. With SPHERE Institute, I've supported federal health policy work; with the Stop AAPI Hate coalition, I've used spatial analytics to map COVID-era anti-Asian incidents and identify vulnerable communities. This kind of collaboration, built on co-produced questions, transparent methods, and decision-ready briefs, maps, and dashboards, helps funders and practitioners act quickly and credibly, and leaves behind open resources others can build on.

### 🌍 High-Tech, Regional Development & Migration
A second strand of my work examines how today's knowledge economy shapes migration, regional development, and inequality. I study disparities in STEM and in superstar regions by gender and nativity, and aim to inform policies that widen opportunity and make growth more inclusive.
