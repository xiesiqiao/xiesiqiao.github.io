First-author articles

  1. 2026 Xie, S., & Connor, D. S. STEMming from where? Spatial and demographic income disparities in the U.S. technology workforce. Annals of the American Association of Geographers.

    Women in STEM earn about 15% less than men. Accounting for personal characteristics and labor-market context widens the gap rather than closing it, and it persists inside the leading tech regions.

    PDFJournal page

  2. 2023 Xie, S. Global education trajectories and inequality: STEM workers from China to the US. Journal of Ethnic and Migration Studies, 49(18), 4699–4721.

    China-born STEM workers educated only in China earn 5–25% less than otherwise similar peers with some U.S. education.

    PDFJournal page

  3. 2022 Xie, S., Li, W., & Tan, Y. Vulnerability, infection, and anti-discrimination among Asian Americans and COVID-19: Explorations into race and health intersections in California. In S. D. Brunn & D. Gilbreath (Eds.), COVID-19 and a World of Ad Hoc Geographies. Springer.

    An Asian American–specific social vulnerability index, mapped against infection and reported anti-Asian incidents across California.

    PDFBook page

Under review

  • Xie, S., Gutmann, M., Leyk, S., Berg, A., Hoose, S., Hollister, L., Kwon, H., & Connor, D. Mapping neighborhood patterns in the U.S. mid-life mortality crisis: A sequence analysis.Under review, SSM – Population Health

    Six million death certificates from 13 states, 2005–2021, sorted into seven neighborhood mortality trajectories. Most of the variation is within counties, which county averages miss.

    Preprint on SSRN

  • Xie, S., Parker, E., et al. Left behind, or who's left? Local composition, local context, and state policy in the rural midlife mortality penalty.Under review, Rural Sociology

    Midlife mortality fell in urban and suburban tracts from 1995 to 2019 but rose in the most remote ones. Who lives in remote places, and the resources around them, account for the gap in all eight states studied.

  • Xie, S., & Connor, D. Highly skilled immigrants serve as a talent lifeline for lagging U.S. regions.Under review, Humanities and Social Sciences Communications

    Lagging U.S. regions rely on foreign-born STEM workers about twice as much as leading regions to keep their high-skill workforce.

  • Connor, D. S., … Xie, S., et al. Community wealth shapes cognitive health among older adults.Revise and resubmit, Science Advances

    Across nearly two million adults, living in a wealthier community is linked to lower risk of cognitive impairment, beyond a person's own wealth, and most of all in the poorest communities.

    Preprint

  • Kemeny, T., Connor, D., Suss, J., Xie, S., Jang, J., & Gu, Z. Spatial wealth inequality in the United States.Under review, Journal of Economic Geography

    Using household net worth across 722 commuting zones since 1960, wealth turns out to be over 60% more spatially concentrated than income.

    Preprint

  • Gutmann, M., Berg, A., Hollister, L., Hoose, S., Kwon, H., Leyk, S., & Xie, S. Big data for health research: Understanding trends in working-age mortality at very fine spatial scales.Under review, Scientific Data

    How confidential death records from fifteen states were acquired, geocoded, and harmonized to study working-age mortality below the county level.

    Preprint on SSRN

  • Kwon, H., Xie, S., et al. The multi-dimensional scale effects in mortality analysis: Understanding the trade-off between spatial, temporal, and thematic detail.Under review, Spatial Demography

    How the choice of geographic unit, time window, and cause-of-death detail changes what mortality analyses find.
